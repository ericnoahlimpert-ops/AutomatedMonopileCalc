Verification

The implementation is verified against the published results of the PISA design model (Burd et al. 2020) and of Thieken et al. (2015). Every verification run has its own input folder in Inputs/Verification/ and its results in results/verification/; each group contains a short evaluation:

  • PISA_Verification.md
  • Thieken_Verification.md
  • Tott-Buswell_Verification.md
  • PISA_Clay_Verification.md
  • PISA_Layered_Verification.md

1. PISA sand model (Burd et al. 2020)

Reference: Table 7 of the paper gives exact values of its 1D design model (GDSM) for the calibration piles C1 (D = 10 m, L = 20 m) and C4 (D = 10 m, L = 60 m), t = 91 mm, h = 50 m, D_R = 75 % (Dunkirk sand, γ' = 10.09 kN/m³, G0 after Hardin & Black with B = 875, e0 = 0.629, K0 = 0.4).

Settings: displacement control to v_G = 1 m (5 levels) and to v_G = 1 mm (1 level), DIAMETER_REFERENCE = "mid", SHEAR_FACTOR = 0.5, material PISA_STEEL (E = 200 GPa), 0.5 m elements as in Table 7.

PileValueThis implementationTable 7Difference
C1H_ult at v_G = 1 m25.51 MN25.55 MN−0.17 %
C4H_ult at v_G = 1 m174.10 MN174.34 MN−0.14 %
C1H_sd at v_G = 1 mm0.5382 MN0.5385 MN−0.06 %
C4H_sd at v_G = 1 mm0.7547 MN0.7556 MN−0.12 %

All levels converged (5–13 Abaqus runs per level). The remaining differences come from the discretisation (springs lumped at the nodes, Abaqus B31 beam; the paper integrates the soil reactions with four Gauss points per element). The GDSM functions (Table 6), the conic function (eqs. 2–5) and G0 (eq. 7) were also checked term by term against the paper.

1.1 Load-displacement curves (Fig. 13)

To check the whole curve and not only the reference points, Fig. 13 (a) and (c) of the paper were recreated. The paper's curves were extracted from the vector graphics of the PDF and scaled with the axis ticks; they are exact to well below 0.1 % (the extracted 1D curve of C1 ends at 25.553 MN at 1 m, Table 7: 25.551 MN). This implementation was run with 20 load levels of 0.05 m (input folders PISA_Verification/C1_curve, C4_curve).

PileThis implementation vs. paper 1D curve (0.05 … 1.0 m)At 1 m vs. Table 7
C1−0.13 … −0.24 %, nearly constant−0.17 %
C4+0.9 … +1.2 %, nearly constant−0.17 %

For C4 the offset comes from the figure itself: the paper's plotted 1D curve ends at 172.52 MN, its own Table 7 gives 174.34 MN for the same model. Against Table 7 both piles deviate by −0.17 % at 1 m. The deviations of this implementation from the 3D finite-element curve (C1: up to −6 % at 0.25–0.4 m) are those of the PISA model itself. Plot, data and script: results/verification/PISA_Verification/Fig13_comparison/.

1.2 Pile diameter

The PISA papers use one diameter D (their pile tables) both for the thin-walled section properties (I = π·D³·t/8, A = π·D·t) and in all formulas. DIAMETER_REFERENCE = "mid" does exactly this with D from pile.csv (Abaqus pipe with outer radius D/2 + t/2). Treating the same D as the outer diameter misses Table 7 clearly:

PileValue"mid""outer"
C1H at 1 mm−0.06 %−1.2 %
C4H at 1 mm−0.12 %−1.6 %
C1H at 1 m−0.17 %−1.2 %
C4H at 1 m−0.14 %−1.6 %

All PISA verifications therefore use "mid" with the D of the papers.

5. PISA clay model (Byrne et al. 2020)

Reference: Table 5 of the paper gives exact values of its 1D model (second-stage parameters) for piles C1 (D = 10 m, L = 20 m) and C4 (D = 10 m, L = 60 m), t = 91 mm, h = 50 m. The soil profile is only given as figures.

  • Exact inputs: σ′_v from γ′ = 11.38 kN/m³, and G0 = 1100·p′ (Table 2 of the paper).
  • Read from figures: K0 from Fig. 19 of Burd et al. (2020b), and s_u from Fig. 4a.

Settings as for sand: mid, κ = 0.5, PISA_STEEL, 0.5 m elements, displacement control. Details in PISA_Clay_Verification.md.

PileValueThis implementationTable 5Difference
C1H_ult at v_G = 1 m12.440 MN12.964 MN−4.0 %
C4H_ult at v_G = 1 m102.19 MN104.03 MN−1.8 %
C1H_sd at v_G = 1 mm0.7203 MN0.7395 MN−2.6 %
C4H_sd at v_G = 1 mm0.8903 MN0.9105 MN−2.2 %

All values are 2–4 % low. Most likely the cause is the figure readings, not the model:

  • Same code, sand: it reproduces the sand model within 0.2 % (section 1).
  • Small displacement: H_sd does not depend on s_u (p = k_p·G0·v in the linear range), so only K0 remains uncertain there.
  • Ultimate: H_ult is governed by s_u.
  • K0 reading matters: with K0 read from the smaller Fig. 4b of the paper, the deviations at 1 mm were −7.1 / −5.1 %.

6. PISA for layered soils (Burd et al. 2020b)

Reference: Table 4 of the paper gives, for 35 layered profiles, the 3D finite-element loads and the ratio ρ = H(1D)/H(3D) at v_G = D/10 000 and D/10. The paper's 1D result is H(1D) = ρ·H(3D); ρ is rounded to two decimals (±0.5 %).

Cases:

  • Exact inputs (Dunkirk sand only): A4, E1A, E1B (pile D2t: D = 8.75 m, t = 150 mm, L/D = 4) and F6 (pile C1t: D = 10 m, t = 150 mm, L/D = 2).
  • Sand over Cowden till (Cowden profile as in section 5): A2 (D2t) and F1 (C1t).

Assessment:

  • Ultimate loads, sand only: within 0.6 %, i.e. within the rounding of ρ. The local application of the reaction curves per layer reproduces the paper's model.
  • Ultimate loads, sand over Cowden till: −0.9 % and −1.5 %, consistent with the Cowden s_u of section 5.
  • Small displacement, pile D2t: about +2 %. This is unexplained.
    • Taking D = 8.75 m as the outer diameter would remove it, but the same convention misses Table 7 of the sand paper by 1.2–1.6 % (section 1.2).
    • F6 (C1t, same wall thickness) deviates only +0.8 %.
